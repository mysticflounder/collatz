This review evaluates the mathematical claims and structural integrity of the paper "Ghost Cycles of the Syracuse Map: 2-Adic Periodic Orbits and the Exceptional Set" by Adam McKenna.

---

### **Abstract & Introduction (Sections 1–2)**

The paper claims to disprove an earlier conjecture that the "exceptional set" $E$ (levels where modular cycles beyond {1} exist) has density zero. It introduces "ghost cycles"—2-adic periodic orbits that project onto modular levels—and asserts that these cycles are true periodic orbits on $\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{odd}$ with negative rational elements. The introduction successfully frames the Collatz problem within a transfer operator framework.

**Assessment:** The high-level claims are bold but logically structured. The identification of $x=3$ expansion against $2^{-v}$ contraction as the "root cause" of spectral obstructions is a strong conceptual anchor. A referee might criticize the reliance on "ghost cycles" as a colloquialism, though the paper eventually defines them rigorously as 2-adic orbits.

### **Transfer Matrix & Spectral Theory (Section 3)**

McKenna defines the transfer matrix $P_k$ and the operator $\mathcal{L}$ on $C(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{odd})$. The central proofs establish $\|\mathcal{L}\| [cite_start]= 2/3$ and $\rho(\mathcal{L}) \le 1/2$. Theorem 1 links the spectrum of $\mathcal{L}$ directly to the union of spectra of finite approximations $P_k$.

**Assessment:** The math here is precise. The correction of the operator norm from $1/3$ to $2/3$ (by accounting for $n \equiv 2 \pmod 3$ preimages) demonstrates necessary rigor . However, a referee would scrutinize the jump from finite $P_k$ eigenvalues to the full spectrum of $\mathcal{L}$ in Theorem 1(e) ; while the density of locally constant functions supports this, the lack of quasi-compactness makes the "closure" argument more delicate than presented .

### **Spectral Obstructions (Sections 4–5)**

Theorem 2 proves that $\mathcal{L}$ does not preserve Lipschitz or Hölder spaces due to "mod-3 oscillation," which prevents the use of Lasota-Yorke inequalities. Theorem 3 shows $\mathcal{L}$ is unbounded on $C(\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{odd}, \mathbb{Q}_2)$, effectively "closing" the Mahler/Amice program for 2-adic function spaces.

**Assessment:** These are the most technically "honest" parts of the paper. Instead of forcing a spectral gap, the author proves why standard tools fail. The proof in Section 5—showing $\|P_k\|_{2-adic}$ grows as $2^k$—is a straightforward but devastating observation for 2-adic analytic approaches. A referee might ask for more detail on why no *other* 2-adic Banach space could potentially work, though Corollary 1 attempts to address this broadly.

### **Ghost Cycles & Persistence (Section 7)**

This section defines ghost types $(L, V, (v_i))$ and distinguishes between Case-(a) (true 2-adic orbits) and Case-(b) (transient modular artifacts). Theorem 5 (Persistence) is the paper's pivot: it proves that Case-(a) ghosts reappear periodically at levels $k \equiv k_0 \pmod p$, where $p = \text{ord}_2(|D|)$.

**Assessment:** The "cycle equation" is standard , but the persistence theorem is a significant contribution to the modular study of Collatz. The distinction between Case-(a) and Case-(b) is well-supported by the digit stabilization visualization (Figure 2). A concern is Conjecture 1 (Universal Case-(a)), which, while verified for 85 million samples, lacks a general proof and is vital for the broader density claims.

### **Density & Spectral Radius (Section 9)**

The paper calculates a lower bound for the density of the exceptional set $\delta(E) \ge 4\%$ based on the $D=-601$ ghost. It proposes a product formula for $\delta(E)$ and argues that $\rho(\mathcal{L})$ could be exactly $1/2$ if the $V=L+1$ family materializes for all $L$.

**Assessment:** The falsification of the density-zero conjecture is mathematically sound *if* the persistence of the $D=-601$ ghost is accepted. However, a referee will likely find the "product formula" in Conjecture 2 speculative. It assumes a form of independence (equidistribution) between different ghost periods that is not proven, even if empirical data for $k=1000$ looks promising.

### **Negative Rationality: Conjecture 4 & Theorem 6 (Section 9.4)**

McKenna conjectures that all $D < 0$ ghost types consist of purely negative rational elements. Theorem 6 provides an unconditional proof for "concentrated" patterns $(1, \dots, 1, e+1)$, using a closed-form formula for the orbit numerators $R_i$.

**Assessment:** Theorem 6 is a high-water mark for the paper's analytical work. The derivation of $R_i$ and the subsequent parity check to confirm Case-(a) status are robust. The "weak point" is extending this to non-concentrated patterns; the paper admits this requires "new control on cancellations" that currently doesn't exist.

### **Fredholm Determinant & Related Work (Sections 10–11)**

The paper notes that for non-exceptional $k$, the Fredholm determinant is simply $1 - z/4$. Section 10 confirms that $P_k$ is nearly nilpotent, with all non-cycle eigenvalues being exactly zero.

**Assessment:** The numerical eigenvalue results (Table 6) are clear and assist in understanding the operator's structure. A referee might find the discussion of the Fredholm determinant brief; more detail on the transition of the determinant's roots as $k$ moves through $E$ would strengthen the connection between the modular and 2-adic operators.

### **Discussion & Conclusion (Section 12)**

The author summarizes the findings, emphasizing the "Archimedean Category" as the only viable path forward for spectral analysis. It acknowledges that while ghost cycles do not solve Collatz, they redefine the spectral constraints of the problem.

**Assessment:** The conclusion is appropriately cautious. The "Iwasawa Analogy" ($\mu \ne 0$) is an interesting but underdeveloped remark that a math referee might find either visionary or distracting. The primary concern remains "materialization"—the paper proves Case-(a) ghosts *persist* if they appear, but it cannot yet prove that all ghost types *must* appear at some $k$.
